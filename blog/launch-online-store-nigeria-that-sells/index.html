Ecommerce in Nigeria: How to Launch an Online Store That Actually Sells

By Tobi Arowosegbe · Ecommerce developer, Lagos · Updated August 2026

Launching an online store is the easy part now — WooCommerce or Shopify make setup fast. Getting it to actually convert visitors into paying customers is where most Nigerian ecommerce launches quietly underperform. Here's what actually moves the needle.

Payment setup: don't make this an afterthought

Integrate Paystack and/or Flutterwave properly from day one — both support cards, bank transfer, and USSD, which matters because a meaningful share of Nigerian shoppers still prefer bank transfer or USSD over card payment. A store offering card-only checkout is quietly losing sales it never sees in any analytics dashboard.

Trust signals that actually matter

  • Real product photos, not just stock images or manufacturer photos everyone else selling the same product also uses.
  • Clear, visible contact information — a phone number and WhatsApp link specifically; Nigerian shoppers frequently want to confirm a purchase or ask a question before paying, especially from a store they haven't bought from before.
  • Honest delivery timelines and costs, shown before checkout, not sprung on the customer at the last step — surprise shipping costs are one of the biggest cart-abandonment triggers anywhere, and Nigeria is no exception.
  • Visible return/refund policy, even a simple one — its absence quietly raises doubt, especially for first-time buyers on an unfamiliar store.

Mobile checkout, tested properly

The large majority of Nigerian ecommerce traffic is mobile. Actually complete a full test purchase on your own phone, on mobile data (not wifi), before considering the store "launched." Checkout forms that are painful to fill on a phone keyboard lose sales silently — there's no error message, the customer just leaves.

Product pages that actually sell

  • Specific, useful descriptions — not just a copy-pasted manufacturer spec sheet everyone else selling the item also uses verbatim.
  • Multiple product images from different angles, and video where it genuinely helps (clothing, gadgets, anything with texture or fit questions).
  • Clear stock status — "in stock" vs "3 left" vs "out of stock," accurately maintained, not left stale.

Technical SEO for product discovery

If you want organic (non-paid) traffic to actually find your products, set up Google Merchant Center with a clean, error-free product feed — I cover exactly how to fix the most common feed errors in a separate guide. Ecommerce SEO without Merchant Center is leaving a major, free discovery channel unused.

The mistake that kills more stores than anything else

Launching, then going quiet — no content updates, no marketing push, no fixing the checkout issues real customers actually hit. An ecommerce store isn't a one-time build; it's an ongoing operation that needs traffic driven to it and friction removed continuously after launch.
