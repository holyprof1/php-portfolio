Technical SEO Checklist for Nigerian Businesses (2026)

By Tobi Arowosegbe · Technical SEO specialist, Lagos · Updated August 2026

Most SEO advice online is written for markets with fast broadband, English-only competition, and mature Google Business Profiles. Nigerian businesses are competing under different conditions — slower average mobile connections, shared hosting that throttles under load, and a lot of local competitors who have never touched technical SEO at all. That last part is actually good news: fixing the basics puts you ahead of most local competition immediately.

This is the checklist I actually run through on client sites, in the order I run through it.

1. Confirm Google can actually crawl and index your site

  • Check robots.txt isn't accidentally blocking Googlebot (common after a developer copies a staging site's robots.txt to production).
  • Submit an XML sitemap in Google Search Console and confirm it's being read without errors.
  • Use the URL Inspection tool in Search Console on your 5 most important pages and confirm each shows "URL is on Google."
  • Search site:yourdomain.com in Google and sanity-check the page count against what you'd expect.
If you're running multiple subdomains (a common setup for agencies and portfolio-style sites), verify the root domain as a Domain property in Search Console via a DNS TXT record. That one verification covers every subdomain automatically, instead of fighting with separate HTML meta tags per subdomain.

2. Structured data (schema markup)

Add JSON-LD structured data for whatever your page actually is — LocalBusiness for a physical business, Product for ecommerce, Article for blog content, Person or ProfessionalService for a personal brand or freelancer. This doesn't guarantee rich results, but it removes ambiguity for Google about what your page represents, which matters more as AI-driven search summaries become a bigger share of traffic.

3. Core Web Vitals, tested on Nigerian conditions

Testing your site on office wifi and testing it on the network conditions your actual visitors use are two different exercises. Run PageSpeed Insights and pay attention to the mobile score specifically, not the desktop one. Common fixes that matter disproportionately here:

  • Compress and lazy-load images — oversized hero images are the single biggest speed killer on Nigerian hosting.
  • Avoid loading five different Google Fonts weights when two would do.
  • Use a caching plugin (WP Rocket, LiteSpeed Cache, or W3 Total Cache on WordPress) if your host supports it.
  • Check your hosting isn't oversold — a ₦15,000/year shared hosting plan serving a business site under real traffic is a common, quiet cause of slow Time to First Byte.

4. On-page basics that get skipped

  • Every page has a unique, descriptive <title> and meta description — not the same one copy-pasted across pages.
  • One <h1> per page, and it actually contains the primary keyword.
  • Internal links between related pages using descriptive anchor text, not just "click here."
  • Image alt text describes the image, ideally including relevant context (this also helps AI-driven and image search visibility).

5. Location and local search signals

If you serve a specific city or region in Nigeria, say so explicitly on the page — in the title, in a heading, and in the body copy. "Web developer in Lagos" ranks for different, more winnable searches than just "web developer." Pair this with a complete, verified Google Business Profile if you have any physical presence or take local clients.

6. Mobile usability

Over 80% of web traffic in Nigeria is mobile. Test tap target sizes, check that menus don't break on smaller screens, and confirm forms are usable with a phone keyboard. Google's mobile-first indexing means the mobile version of your site is what actually gets ranked, not the desktop one.

7. HTTPS, redirects, and canonical tags

  • Site loads on https:// with a valid certificate (most Nigerian hosts include free SSL now — check it's actually being used, not just installed).
  • http:// and the non-www version both redirect cleanly to your canonical URL, with no redirect chains.
  • Every page has a self-referencing canonical tag to prevent duplicate content issues, especially common on WooCommerce sites with filter/sort URL parameters.

8. For ecommerce: product feed health

If you're running Google Merchant Center for a Nigerian ecommerce store, product feed errors are one of the most common and most damaging technical SEO issues I see — they silently stop your products from showing in Shopping results. I cover this in detail in a separate guide on fixing Merchant Center errors.
