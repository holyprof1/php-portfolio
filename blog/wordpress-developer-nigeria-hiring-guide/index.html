WordPress Developer in Nigeria: A Complete Hiring Guide (2026)

By Tobi Arowosegbe · Full stack & WordPress developer, Lagos · Updated August 2026

If you have ever searched for a "WordPress developer in Nigeria," you already know the problem: the range of quotes you get back is enormous. One person quotes ₦50,000. Another quotes ₦800,000 for what looks like the same job. Both of them might be right, or both might be wrong, depending on what they are actually planning to deliver.

I have built and fixed WordPress sites for businesses in Nigeria, Ghana, and remotely for clients across the US and Europe for over seven years, so this guide is written from the hiring side of that experience — what separates a developer who will hand you a stable site from one who will hand you a problem with a live domain attached to it.

What "WordPress developer" actually covers

WordPress work in Nigeria usually falls into one of four buckets, and pricing differs a lot between them:

  • Theme setup and customization — installing a purchased or free theme, adjusting colors, layout, and content. Fastest and cheapest.
  • Custom theme or plugin development — building functionality that does not exist off the shelf, using PHP, the WordPress hooks system, and often a page builder like Elementor underneath.
  • WooCommerce / ecommerce builds — product catalogs, payment gateway integration (Paystack, Flutterwave, or international gateways), shipping logic, and inventory.
  • Maintenance and fixes — malware cleanup, broken plugin conflicts, migrations, speed optimization, and security hardening on an existing site.

Get clarity on which of these you actually need before you ask for quotes. A lot of mismatched pricing complaints come from comparing a "theme setup" quote against a "custom build" quote as if they were the same job.

Realistic pricing in Nigeria (2026)

These are working ranges based on active project pricing, not list prices from an agency site. Actual cost depends heavily on scope, timeline, and whether content/copy is already prepared.
  • Basic business website (5–7 pages, existing theme): ₦150,000 – ₦400,000
  • Custom-designed business website: ₦400,000 – ₦1,200,000
  • WooCommerce store (small catalog, one payment gateway): ₦350,000 – ₦900,000
  • Ongoing maintenance retainer: ₦25,000 – ₦100,000/month depending on scope
  • Hacked site cleanup / emergency fix: ₦40,000 – ₦150,000 depending on damage

If a quote is dramatically below these ranges, ask what is being cut — usually it is hosting quality, security hardening, mobile testing, or post-launch support.

Questions to ask before you pay a deposit

  1. "Can I see 2–3 live sites you built, not just screenshots?" A live URL you can click through on your phone tells you far more than a portfolio image.
  2. "Who owns the hosting and domain once it's done?" You should own both, in your own accounts, not the developer's. This is the single most common way freelance relationships in Nigeria go bad — the site gets held hostage on someone else's hosting account.
  3. "What happens if a plugin breaks after launch?" Get this in writing, even informally over WhatsApp or email. A 30-day post-launch fix window is a fair, common standard.
  4. "Are you using a nulled/pirated theme or plugin?" Nulled premium plugins are a leading cause of malware infections on Nigerian WordPress sites. If the developer can't tell you plainly, treat that as a red flag.
  5. "How will the site perform on 3G/4G, not just your office wifi?" A lot of WordPress sites built and tested only on fast connections load painfully slowly on the networks most Nigerian visitors actually use.

Red flags that predict a broken site

  • No written scope — just a WhatsApp back-and-forth with no agreed page list or feature list.
  • Refusal to give you admin access to your own WordPress dashboard "for security."
  • Full payment requested upfront with no milestone structure.
  • Heavy reliance on a single all-in-one page builder plugin with no explanation of what happens if that plugin's subscription lapses.
  • No mention of backups, SSL, or basic security (login limiting, firewall) at any point in the conversation.

Where to actually find good WordPress developers in Nigeria

Referrals from someone whose site you can already see and click through beat cold outreach almost every time. Beyond that, Upwork and Fiverr profiles with verifiable, completed jobs and real client reviews are more reliable than local "agency" websites, which are sometimes built by the same person pitching you and don't reflect actual client delivery.
