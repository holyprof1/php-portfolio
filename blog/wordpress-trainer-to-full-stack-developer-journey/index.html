From WordPress Trainer to Full Stack Developer: My 7-Year Journey

By Tobi Arowosegbe · Updated August 2026

I didn't start out writing React components or debugging Laravel migrations. I started out teaching people WordPress — how to install a theme, how to structure a page, how to not break a site while trying to fix it. That teaching background turns out to shape almost everything about how I approach development work today.

Starting with WordPress and training

Early on, most of my work was hands-on WordPress delivery and training interns and students in web development and digital marketing basics through BTO Academy and similar programs. Teaching forces you to actually understand something well enough to explain it simply — you can't hide behind jargon when a student is watching you debug a broken shortcode live. That habit of explaining the "why," not just executing the "what," stuck with me.

Moving into PHP and Laravel

WordPress work naturally pulled me deeper into PHP — fixing plugin conflicts meant reading real PHP code, and eventually building custom functionality meant writing it. From there, Laravel was the obvious next step for anything beyond what WordPress's plugin ecosystem could reasonably handle: booking systems, learning platforms like Docenti LMS, and custom business logic that didn't fit a CMS.

Frontend and the move to React

As client work started requiring more interactive, app-like interfaces — dashboards, product screens, real-time data — I moved into React and modern frontend tooling. Working as a Frontend Developer on international remote teams (including Kyivstar) and later on fintech-facing product work at PayVessel pushed that further, into the kind of frontend engineering where performance, state correctness, and edge-case handling actually matter under real usage.

Where technical SEO fit in

SEO wasn't a separate career track for me — it came directly out of building sites for real businesses that needed to actually be found. A beautifully built website that nobody can find in Google isn't doing its job. That practical need turned into a deliberate specialty: indexing fixes, structured data, Merchant Center troubleshooting, and the technical side of visibility.

What seven years across all of this actually gives you

The advantage of coming up through training, WordPress, PHP, frontend, and SEO in that order — rather than specializing narrowly from day one — is that I understand a project end to end: how it's built, how it's marketed, and how it actually gets found. Most client problems aren't purely technical or purely marketing problems; they're both at once, and having lived on both sides makes the fixes more complete.

Whether you need a developer who also understands visibility, or someone who can take a project from build to launch to ranking, that's the work I do.
